Why profiles

On the call I described an envelope with our names on it. We open it and there’s a business model inside that makes us both say “this is perfect.” It’s probably not something new. It’s what we already do and want, put in a shape we can act on. Two honest profiles are the raw material for that.

Three steps

  1. You read mine in section Two and write yours in section Three.
  2. I put both profiles together and write the questions for you that come out of them.
  3. From your answers we get a few ideas for how we work together, and pick what to try.

The prompt to paste into your AI

The nine headings

  • Background and past work: Where you’ve worked, what you’ve built or run, roles you’ve held.
  • Hard skills: Tools you use and things you can do yourself today, without hiring it out.
  • Strengths: What people come to you for. What you do better than most.
  • Weaknesses and blind spots: Where you drop the ball, what you put off, what someone else usually covers for you.
  • How I work best: Pace, structure, alone or together, how you like to get information and make decisions.
  • What energizes me, what drains me: The work you’d do for free, and the work you dread.
  • Time I can give right now: What already fills your week, and when that changes.
  • What I need from the next thing: Income, how much risk, how much travel, how long you’d wait for it to pay.
  • Ideas I’m already drawn to: Anything you keep coming back to, finished or half-formed.

Strengths

  • My superpower: asking questions and working out solutions for process and automation problems.
  • Organizing. Turning a messy process into a system people actually follow.
  • Scope. I’d rather cut something on purpose than carry work nobody priced.
  • Taking your bird’s-eye first pass and filling in the detail.
  • Follow-through. I record calls and keep track of what each side said they’d do.
  • Stepping up when nobody else will. That’s how I became BNI president.
  • Building fast and learning by running it, not by planning it.

Weaknesses and blind spots

  • I’d rather be in the background than lead the room. I speak up on what I’m passionate about.
  • I go down the rabbit hole. I’ll get organized about how to get organized.
  • I can start building before the market has said yes.
  • I carry a lot of threads at once. A new one needs a clear scope or it waits.
  • Asking for the money doesn’t come as naturally as doing the work.

How I work best

  • One thing at a time, finished before the next.
  • Something built and tried beats a plan reviewed. If it’s wrong, we change it.
  • Two modes: brainstorm, where I want wide and outside-the-box, and build, where I know what I want and just execute.
  • Questions written down up front, so the other person can answer when it suits them.
  • In the same room for the big thinking, apart for the doing.
