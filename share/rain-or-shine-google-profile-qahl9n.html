Google treats Rain or Shine as a new business

When a business keeps its name and gets a new owner, Google lets the profile and its reviews carry over. When the name changes completely, Google counts it as a new business. Callin’ All Paws to Rain or Shine is a complete change.

i

Renaming the old profile could get it shut down

Taking over Callin’ All Paws and renaming it would break that rule. Google can suspend a profile for it, and the old reviews were written about someone else’s grooming, so they can’t honestly count for you anyway.

i

No logins or passwords needed from anyone

You don’t need anything from Alisha or her friend. Rain or Shine gets its own profile under your Google account, and the old listing gets marked closed, which anyone can suggest on Google Maps.

i

What you end up with

A profile that’s yours from day one, under your own name, that nobody else can lock you out of. Every review on it is about your work. It starts at zero, and the steps in section Five are how it grows.

8

Verify the business

Answered

Google picks the method. It may be a phone or text code, an email, a live video call or a postcard. For a video call:

  1. Be at the shop during your business hours.
  2. Start outside: the sign, the street, the businesses next door.
  3. Walk in using your keys.
  4. Show the grooming table, tub, dryers and tools.

Postcards can take up to 14 days. If Google offers anything faster, take it.

Who can and can’t review

Google removes reviews from the owner, staff, family and friends, and can flag a profile that has them. Reviews about the website don’t count either. The first reviews need to come from real customers about a real groom. It’s slower at the start, and it’s the only kind that lasts.

Also off-limits: discounts or freebies in exchange for a review, and only asking the customers you know are happy. Ask everyone.
